## The fixes, one entry at a time (requirement, failing test, code)

### CR-017 The verdict follows the figure shown
- R-024 (and R-078's wording): the verdict is `GOOD` when the weakest ratio, as shown with one
  decimal, reaches `Th.SwampingRatioOk`; the summary's extra noise has one decimal.
- Tests in `SeriesTests` (`R024_...`): `SeriesReport.SummaryRows` on a result whose weakest
  ratio is 9.96 gives `GOOD` beside `10.0` (`MARGINAL` today), 9.94 gives `MARGINAL` beside
  `9.9`; the text carries `4.9 %`; the `what it means` row's bound equals the one worked out
  from `Th.SwampingRatioOk`.
- Code: `SeriesReport.Exposure` compares the formatted figure parsed back
  (`RunReport.Shown(value, decimals)`, new), so the digits and the verdict cannot disagree;
  `NoiseRows` works its `5` out from the threshold with the formula `NoiseAnalyzer` uses for
  the extra noise (made a named function there if it is inline).

### CR-018 Tables that sit under their headers
- R-003 and the series table's requirement: both languages' headers are built with the rows'
  widths; a column is as wide as its widest cell, never narrower than today.
- Tests (`R003_...`, the series table's ID): for both tables and both languages every header
  word starts (left columns) or ends (right columns) where its cells do, on the classes
  fixture (`la limita`), and on rows with a pause of 1000 s and a `10/10 de la 100` cell.
- Code: a small `TextTable` in `Report/` (columns: header, side, least width; `Write` pads
  header and rows alike); `SeriesReport.Write` and `InventoryReport.WriteTable` use it with
  `T(...)` headers. The English output stays byte for byte where no cell overflows.
- Not here: `- mm` for a missing focal length (CR-033).

### CR-019 One frame, one dark
- R-081/R-082 (the count words): the texts of C; R-082 covers the `ATENTIE` line.
- Tests: one per text of C, both languages (`SeriesReport.SummaryRows` and `FocusRows` on
  small results, the pattern row with one dark, `FrameRun.Run` on a one-light session, the
  header with 20 unread frames).
- Code: `SeriesReport.SummaryRows`, `FocusRows`, `PatternRow` (through the existing `Count`
  helper where it fits), `FrameRun.Run`, `InventoryReport.WriteHeader` (`RoCount`).

### CR-020 Two rows for the marginal frames (answer 2)
- R-085, R-090, R-081: the `marginal` row lists the frames marginal by their elongation (flag
  `MarginalTrail`, class marginal); `marginal for another reason` lists the marginal frames
  with neither that flag nor a tail (flags `Soft`, `Clouds`), and is there only when one
  exists; texts D.
- Tests in `SeriesTests` (`R085_...`): on the classes fixture the rows read as in D, in both
  languages; the eight-frame pattern keeps three rows; the explanation's second paragraph.
- Code: `SeriesReport.TrailRows` (two listers in place of `MarginalWithoutTail`),
  `RejectedExplanation`.

### CR-021 No minus on a zero (answer 3)
- New R-092; R-036's example (`(-0 %)`) follows it.
- Tests (`R092_...`, `RunReportTests`): `F(-0.0004, 3)` is `0.000`, `F(-0.04, 1)` is `0.0`,
  `F(-0.06, 1)` stays `-0.1`; a signed zero is `+0`, `+0.00`; one test per report line of E
  that a fixture reaches.
- Code: `RunReport.F` drops the minus when every digit it wrote is 0 (decided on the written
  digits, as FlatCheck's `Numbers.Signed` does); `SeriesReport.Signed` puts `+` unless the
  written figure starts with `-`; the trail angle formatted in `SeriesAnalyzer` (`F0`) and the
  window's chart labels (`Charts.Num`) go through `RunReport.F`. The CSV keeps its numbers as
  they are: it is read by programs.
