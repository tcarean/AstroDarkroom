# Excerpt of the research "is Siril's -opt advice for older or newer cameras" (2026-11-20)

The points below are copied word for word from the research result of 2026-11-20 (a search of
Siril's own texts, of other programs' practice and of camera tests, each point re-checked by a
second reader in that research). Only the points `conclusions.md` cites are here; the full
file sat in a temporary folder. `[Siril]`, `[Practice]` and `[Camera]` are that file's own
labels. Nothing here was measured in this repository.

- [Siril] FAQ entry 'My camera is not cooled, what does that change?': the stock scripts assume darks taken at the lights' temperature and exposure. That does not hold for DSLRs and uncooled astro cameras, especially on long sessions, so use dark optimisation with the bias or a synthetic offset removed from darks and lights. No criterion by camera age, CCD/CMOS or amp glow. Source: Siril website, official docs (entry by Cyril Richard). In the site source since at least 2021-01-03; steps reworded by Vincent Hourdin 2022-03-08 and for the calibrate command 2023-08-11; untouched by the 2025-04 and 2026-03 edits. https://siril.org/faq/ ; history https://gitlab.com/free-astro/siril-web/-/commits/master/content/faq/index.md
- [Siril] The French version of the same entry names exposures over 30 s instead of long sessions. Same bias step, no age criterion. Source: Siril website, official docs; entry undated, footer 2026; re-read 2026-11-20. https://siril.org/fr/faq/
- [Siril] Manual 1.4.4: dark optimisation is for darks not taken in optimal conditions. Two modes, auto-evaluation and exposure ratio; needs a bias-subtracted master dark; no camera type named. Its only worked example is a cooled CCD (FLI ProLine 4240): a 600 s master dark on 60 s lights gave k0 = 0.110 against 0.1. Source: Siril manual, official docs, 1.4.4, page last edited 2026-04-23. https://siril.readthedocs.io/en/stable/preprocessing/calibration.html
- [Siril] The stock scripts never use -opt. The bias is already inside the dark; a bias is needed with darks only for -opt. Source: Cyril Richard (Siril developer), 2020-04-14, https://gitlab.com/free-astro/siril/-/issues/465 ; same point 2021-03-18, https://discuss.pixls.us/t/23814/4
- [Siril] Vincent Hourdin backed -opt for DSLRs. In 2020 he reported good results with DSLR cameras (https://discuss.pixls.us/t/20361/8). On 2020-07-13 he told a Nikon D5000 user to make sure he used it; that user shot 180 s ISO 1600 lights at 284-285 K, with darks at 288 K (https://discuss.pixls.us/t/how-far-to-process-an-image-m13/19152/4 ; posts 1 and 4 re-read 2026-11-20). Role: pixls.us vinvin, profile 'Siril co-developer'.
- [Siril] Vincent Hourdin, 2020-07-23: -opt is no use when bias and dark give the same level, as on some high-quality cooled cameras with very low dark current, because the bias-subtracted dark is then almost null. In other cases he thinks it fairly safe. Role: Siril co-developer. https://discuss.pixls.us/t/19270/5
- [Siril] The only age remarks, both by Vincent Hourdin, with no reason given. 2024-04-23, to a Canon EOS R5 user: worth trying, but it often fails with recent CMOS cameras (https://discuss.pixls.us/t/strange-lines-and-circles-in-images/43279/4). 2024-12-02: most modern cameras will not support it (https://discuss.pixls.us/t/stacking-error-mentioning-fwhm-when-not-relevant/46849/2); in that thread (1.3 s subs) post 4 says he is not familiar with modern cameras. No other team member said this, and the FAQ was not changed afterwards.
- [Siril] Adrian Knagg-Baugh, 2025-11-26, to a user with uncooled classic DSLRs and recent Olympus bodies whose sensor temperature drifts: he said (hedged) that auto-evaluation exists for exactly this, that the exposure mode handles other exposure times, and that both need bias-subtracted master darks. Cyril Richard closed the request on 2026-05-02 because the feature exists. Role: Siril developers. https://gitlab.com/free-astro/siril/-/issues/1861
- [Practice] DSS's dark optimisation is an entropy-minimising factor between 0 and 1, meant mainly for darks taken at the wrong temperature (DeepSkyStacker official page, server date 2019-09-03, http://deepskystacker.free.fr/english/technical.htm). The method was designed for uncooled CCD cameras with high dark current, which doubles about every 5 K; plain subtraction works best on temperature-controlled high-end CCDs (Goesele, Heidrich, Seidel, academic paper, 2001-01-25, https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2001/goesele2001a/goesele.2001a.pdf).
- [Practice] ZWO tells users of its CMOS cameras to switch dark optimisation off in DSS, PixInsight and similar programs, because scaling leaves remnant or inverted amp glow. Its DSLR-type sensors (ASI071/094/128) show little glow. Source: ZWO (manufacturer), 2018-04-03. https://www.zwoastro.com/2018/04/03/what-is-amp-glow/
- [Camera] On-sensor dark-current suppression appeared in Canons from about 2008, and later in entry-level models. It blocks the dark level and pattern but not the noise. Clark lists optical-black reference subtraction among the possible mechanisms. He calls scaling darks only a partial fix, and says not all new cameras have suppression. Source: Roger N. Clark (recognised expert): https://clarkvision.com/articles/dark-current-suppression-technology/ (2016-05-25, updated 2026-05-19); https://clarkvision.com/articles/characteristics-of-best-cameras-and-lenses-for-nightscape-astro-photography/ (2016-03-14, updated 2024-05-26); https://clarkvision.com/articles/technology-advancements-for-low-light-long-exposure-imaging/ (2017-01-17, updated 2025-07-12).
- [Camera] The newest glow-free cooled CMOS cameras have tiny dark current. ASI2600: about 0.0022 e-/s per pixel at 0 °C. QHY268: 0.0005 at -20 °C, with zero amp glow. Source: ZWO and QHYCCD (manufacturers), undated pages read 2026-11-20: https://www.zwoastro.com/product/new-asi2600mm-mc-pro/ and https://www.qhyccd.com/astronomical-camera-qhy268/
- [Camera] Measured the same way, newer cameras are not reliably lower. At 20 °C ambient, from differenced 5-min darks, the Canons 300D to 600D, the 7D Mark II, the Nikon D7000 and the Sony A7S/A7RII all started in about the same range and rose 2-9x as the camera warmed. The 2003-2006 Canons were not worse, and the 2015 A7RII ended among the highest (Mark Shelley, amateur sensor tester, 2018-01-13, https://stargazerslounge.com/blogs/entry/2004-d7500-has-high-thermal-pattern-noise/). The 2017 Canon 200D had the highest dark current Shelley had tested, and the D5300 under half of it. Nikon's filtering does not remove all fixed pattern, so darks still help on the D5300 (Shelley, 2018-09-25, https://www.dpreview.com/forums/post/61706568). The 2017 D7500 showed more thermal pattern than the 2013 D5300 (MikeODay, forum user, 2018-01-04, same Stargazers Lounge entry).
- [Camera] D5300 NEF darks are cut off at 588 against a black level of 600, in the file itself. Source: Iliah Borg (LibRaw), 2018-04-17/18. https://libraw.org/node/2362
