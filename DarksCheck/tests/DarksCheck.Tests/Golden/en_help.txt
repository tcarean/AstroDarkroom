Usage: darkscheck <session folder or folder of darks> [--against DIR] [--out DIR] [--threads N] [--max-frames N]
       darkscheck <folder> --inventory

  The Siril session folder: the darks are taken from darks\ (or from the folder
  itself, when there is no darks\ and no other session subfolder), the biases
  from biases\ and the lights from lights\, both optional. Camera raw
  (.nef, .cr2, .cr3, .arw, .dng, .raf, .pef) or FITS. Nothing is written into the folder.

  No options:   reads and measures the biases, the darks and the lights and prints the
                report: light leaks, the camera's warming, the noise of each frame, hot
                pixels, what is the same in every frame, dead and saturated pixels, how
                many darks are enough, how well the dark fits the lights, the index and
                the label, one table for the darks and one for the lights.
  --against DIR another folder of darks (a library or a session): its index and by how
                many degrees the session's darks are warmer or colder than it; a
                session with lights but no darks takes its darks as its own.
  --out DIR     writes into DIR the report, a CSV with every figure for the darks,
                the biases and the lights, and the maps (darkscheck-report.txt, darkscheck-darks.csv,
                darkscheck-biases.csv, darkscheck-lights.csv, darkscheck-map-dark.png, ...).
  --threads N   how many frames are read at the same time (default: half the
                cores of the processor).
  --max-frames N  how many frames are held in memory (default 60, 48 MB each at 24 MP) and how many lights are measured;
                if there are more, only some are read, evenly spaced (one in two, for
                example), and the rest are in the table as skipped.
  --inventory   only what the files say, without reading the pixels: the time, the
                exposure and the ISO of each frame, how often the frames were taken and
                the gaps between them, whether the darks match the lights.
  --lang en|ro  the language of the texts: en (English, the default) or ro (Romanian).
  -h, --help    this text

Exit code: 0 when every frame was read, 1 on an error or when a frame could not be read.
