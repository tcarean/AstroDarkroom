    ADU is the unit in which the file writes how bright each pixel is: here whole numbers from 0
    to 65535 (16 bits), with the camera's black at 200. ADU-eq is the same ADU worked out as if
    the frame had been taken at ISO 400 and 120 s, so that you can compare series taken with
    different settings: on this set 1 ADU-eq = 1.00 ADU in the file. The file goes up 1 by 1
    ADU, but the mean of many pixels can come out as a fraction of an ADU, because the noise
    makes some pixels land on one step and others on the next.

    An error in the dark master is not noise. For example: light that got into the camera, or a
    black level moved a little. The master is subtracted from every light, so the error reaches
    the stack whole, however many frames you add. The noise, on the other hand, falls as you add
    frames: 4 times more frames halve it. The dark master, made of 4 frames, still has 1.87 ADU
    of noise per pixel; that is what you compare an error with, not the noise of a single frame.
    On a single pixel, the 3.0 ADU of noise of one dark hides any error below 1 ADU. But the eye
    sees areas, not pixels: on an area of 50 x 50 pixels the noise evens out, and the error
    shows 50 times better than on one pixel. That is why a step of 1 ADU with a straight edge
    shows when you stretch a dark sky hard.

    Against the sky: on a light of this session the sky is at 500.0 ADU above the black, so 1
    ADU is 0.20 % of the sky, about as much as a flat is usually off. In the stack of the 5
    lights the noise per pixel falls to 3.40 ADU if you make it with the mean (4.02 with the
    median). Two noises go into this figure. The lights' noise (6.3 ADU on one light) falls the
    more lights you add. The dark master's noise (1.87 ADU) does not fall, because the same
    master is subtracted from every light. And an error of 1 ADU in the master stays 1 ADU in
    the stack.

    The thresholds: a pixel is hot above 20 ADU-eq. On the map of the Maps tab, a light leak is
    obvious above 1.0 ADU-eq (the threshold of the analysis made by hand). Constant light on a
    side counts above 0.15 ADU-eq, and only if it is more than 3 times what the measurement can
    be off by. Light on a single frame counts above 0.1 ADU-eq, and only if it is more than 5
    times what a frame usually differs from the others. The last two thresholds say 'this is
    real light, measured', not 'it shows in the image': at 0.15 ADU in the file it would barely
    show, at 1.0 ADU it shows. The remedy is the same in both cases: cover the viewfinder and
    take the series again.
